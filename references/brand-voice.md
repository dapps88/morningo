# Morningo — Brand Voice

## Brand in One Sentence
Steady energy. All morning. No crash.

## Positioning (working enemy — placeholder pending real customer research)
The enemy: inconsistent mornings — the crash, not being able to power through, not having a clear, focused, energised start. Simple and literal until real customer language replaces it.

The category promises to unlock, hack, and supercharge you.
Morningo just stops the crash.

## Who It's For
Adults 35–55. Professionals, cyclists, parents — people with things to do. Quiet achievers who don't need everyone to know they're performing. They want to show up, feel sharp, be dependable — not become superhuman.

## Tone of Voice
British. Dry. Observational. Understated. Warm. Intelligent.
Never: silly, sarcastic, trying too hard, "look at us."

Humour rewards recognition — the reader should think "that's true," not "that's funny."

**The rule:** honest and direct, never oversells — restrained rather than folksy, never cold.
**Test line:** "Coffee's fine. It's the 10am crash that isn't."
**Not:** Liquid Death (too aggressive), Oatly (too loud), Duolingo (meme culture)

**Approved voice examples:**
- "Congratulations. You're awake. That's enough self-improvement for one morning."
- "Good mornings don't need a manifesto."
- "Some people have children. We'll keep this simple."
- "Not everything needs a system."
- "Calm is underrated."

## Brand Is / Is Not
**Is:** Calm. Confident. Competent. Warm. Premium. Grounded. Human.
**Is Not:** Gym bro. Tech bro. Biohacker. Wellness guru. Inspirational. Motivational. Aggressive. Performative. Funny for the sake of funny.

## How This Brand Reasons About Copy

**The truth test:** Is this true, not just clever? Would a real person actually say this out loud? Trust the reader — never over-explain or explain the joke. One idea per line, not three benefits at once. Lead with truth, end with the product — never the other way round.

**The psycho-logic test:** People decide emotionally, justify rationally — write for the decision, not the justification. What's the real problem, not the obvious one? (The category thinks the problem is energy; Morningo's real problem, for now, is inconsistency — the crash that undoes an otherwise fine morning.) What does the opposite of the category sound like — and are we living there?

**The distinctiveness test:** Protect the brand's fixed assets — "Calm. Sharp. Ready." Use it consistently rather than reinventing each time. If a line could belong to any other brand, it belongs to none.

**The challenger test:** Don't fight the category on its terms (science, density, credibility) — fight on emotional territory: real life, real people, a morning that just works. Name the enemy (the crash) and reject it visibly.

**The restraint test:** Less on the page reads as more confident — name exactly what it does, when, why (specificity over hyperbole, not "optimise your morning"), and lead with the customer's truth before arriving at the product. What the category leans on that Morningo rejects: hero claims above the fold, before/after transformation promises, celebrity endorsement theatre, fake urgency ("only 3 left"), long ingredient science dumps, aggressive pop-ups or countdown timers, cluttered social proof walls.

## First Principles
1. Category contrast is strategy — everyone else is screaming, our silence is loud
2. We sell permission, not product — permission to keep your morning exactly as it is, just without the crash
3. Restraint is a creative decision, not a lack of ambition
4. Death by good taste is real — don't iterate into safety
