# Product Science Overview
## Morning Energy Supplement — Ingredient Reference File

**Who this is for:** Any part of the AI system writing copy, claims, briefs, or creative work for this product. Read this before writing anything about how the product works. Do not invent mechanisms. Do not reach beyond what's written here. If something isn't in this file, don't claim it.

---

## The Core Idea — What We're Actually Doing

Most energy products do one thing: hit you with caffeine and hope for the best. The result is familiar — a sharp spike, then a drop, then another coffee, then anxiety by 11am.

This product doesn't work like that. It's built around a single idea: **instead of giving you more energy, we give you better energy.** That means a smooth, sustained, calm feeling of being alert and focused — from roughly 30 minutes after you drink it, through to lunch.

To do that, we've built what we internally call an **energy system** — six ingredients, each with a distinct job, working together. Not a scattergun of everything that sounds good. Six specific things, chosen because together they approach the body from five different angles simultaneously.

The felt experience we're engineering: **calm, focused, switched on.** Not wired. Not buzzing. Not crashing. Just — on.

## The Six Ingredients and What They Actually Do

---

### 1. Green Tea Extract
**The fuel. Standardised for both caffeine and EGCG.**

> **In plain terms:** Delivers caffeine more slowly and smoothly than coffee does. Designed to sit alongside your morning coffee, not replace it — a steady layer of focus rather than another spike.

**What it is:** An extract from green tea leaves, standardised to contain a specific amount of caffeine and EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate — a natural compound found in green tea).

**What it does:** Delivers caffeine in a way that's slower and smoother than coffee or synthetic caffeine. The natural matrix of green tea slows the absorption rate, which means the energy curve is less sharp. EGCG adds its own contribution — it has independent research behind it on neuroprotection and supporting blood flow to the brain.

**Why green tea extract specifically:** This product is designed to be taken alongside your morning coffee, not instead of it. Coffee delivers caffeine fast — a sharp spike most people know well. Green tea extract delivers caffeine in a slower, smoother curve. Together they produce a more sustained, layered effect than coffee alone. Synthetic caffeine is faster-absorbed than even coffee, which makes the crash sharper — green tea extract is the cleanest, steadiest caffeine delivery vehicle available.

**The research:** The caffeine mechanism is the most well-understood thing in the entire nootropics literature — thousands of studies, consistent results. EGCG has strong human trial evidence on vascular function and antioxidant activity. The acute cognitive effect of green tea caffeine is well established.

**What it doesn't do:** It's still caffeine. It still has a ceiling. It still has a come-down. That's what the other ingredients exist to manage.

**Dose range to aim for:** 200–300mg of green tea extract standardised to approximately 50% polyphenols, yielding roughly 50–75mg caffeine per serving. This is deliberately lower than a standard coffee (80–150mg). The product is designed to be taken alongside a morning coffee — a typical combined intake of 130–225mg total caffeine sits well within a comfortable range for most adults without pushing into jitteriness. BioPerine's absorption-enhancing effect on caffeine should also be factored in during formulation, which is another reason to err toward the lower end of this range.

**Label language:** "Green tea extract" — completely clean, immediately understood.

### 2. L-Theanine
**The modulator. The thing that makes the caffeine behave.**

> **In plain terms:** Takes the edge off caffeine without taking away the focus. It's the reason this product feels calm and sharp rather than wired and anxious.

**What it is:** An amino acid found almost exclusively in tea leaves. Not a stimulant. Has no effect on its own in terms of energy — its entire job is to change the *shape* of the caffeine response.

**What it does:** In combination with caffeine, L-theanine promotes alpha brainwave activity — the mental state associated with being alert but calm. It reduces caffeine-induced anxiety, jitteriness, and blood pressure spike without reducing the cognitive benefits of caffeine. It's the reason a strong cup of tea feels different to a strong coffee even at equivalent caffeine doses.

**Why it matters:** This is the single ingredient that turns a caffeine product into a *calm focus* product. Without it, we're just another caffeine sachet. With it, the felt experience changes meaningfully.

**The research:** This is one of the strongest pairings in the cognitive supplement literature. Multiple double-blind randomised controlled trials — the gold standard — consistently show that caffeine + L-theanine outperforms caffeine alone on sustained attention, reaction time, and accuracy, while significantly reducing jitteriness and anxiety. The ratio that performs best in research: 1:2 caffeine to theanine. So 100mg caffeine : 200mg theanine.

**What it doesn't do:** It has no legally permitted health claims in the UK under the GB NHC Register. It cannot be labelled as "supporting focus" or "reducing anxiety" on pack without regulatory risk. The mechanism is real; the claim is not permitted. This distinction matters.

**Dose range:** 150–200mg. At this level it's meaningful. Below 100mg it's underwhelming.

**Label language:** "L-theanine" — well recognised by the informed consumer, reads as considered rather than chemical.

### 3. Bilberry Extract
**The blood flow ingredient. Getting more oxygen to the brain.**

> **In plain terms:** Helps get more blood — and therefore more oxygen — to the brain. More fuel arriving where it needs to be, right when you need it.

**What it is:** Extract from *Vaccinium myrtillus* — European bilberry. Standardised to a minimum of 25–36% anthocyanins, the natural pigment compounds responsible for the dark blue-purple colour in berries.

**What it does:** Anthocyanins support nitric oxide production and slow the enzyme that breaks it down. Nitric oxide causes blood vessels to relax and widen — vasodilation. More blood reaches the brain. More oxygen. More glucose. The brain works with more of what it needs already available to it.

**Why this matters for the product:** Caffeine and L-theanine work on the neurotransmitter and brainwave side. Bilberry works on the *delivery* side — it's making sure the brain is better supplied with blood while the other ingredients are tuning how that brain fires. Different mechanism, complementary outcome.

**The research:** The anthocyanin / nitric oxide / vasodilation pathway is well established. Human studies using brain imaging have shown increased prefrontal blood flow within 1–2 hours of consuming anthocyanin-rich extracts. Spatial working memory and processing speed improvements have been demonstrated in acute studies. However — and this is important — most of the strongest cognitive research used New Zealand blackcurrant extract (CurraNZ), not bilberry specifically. Bilberry shares the same mechanism but has a less specific cognitive research base. Its strongest evidence is on vascular health, retinal circulation, and antioxidant activity. We should position it accordingly.

**What to claim vs. what to avoid:** "Contributes to normal blood flow" is permissible territory. "Improves cognitive performance" directly from bilberry alone is a claim the research doesn't robustly support for this specific ingredient. Don't overreach here.

**Dose range:** 300–500mg of standardised extract.

**Label language:** "Bilberry extract" — sounds botanical, natural, considered. No explanation required.

**Sourcing note:** UK/European supply is straightforward. Standardised bilberry extract from reputable European suppliers is reliable and cost-effective.

### 4. Magnesium L-Threonate
**The neural primer. Getting the brain ready to receive what the other ingredients are delivering.**

> **In plain terms:** Most magnesium stops at the door. This form actually gets into the brain. It primes the neural environment so the rest of the stack has something to work with.

**What it is:** A specific form of magnesium bonded to threonate — a compound that allows it to cross the blood-brain barrier more effectively than other magnesium forms. Most magnesium supplements (magnesium oxide, citrate, glycinate) work primarily in the body. This one is specifically designed to work in the brain.

**Why the form matters:** Magnesium is a cofactor in over 300 enzymatic reactions, including ATP production (how cells make energy). More relevantly here, it regulates NMDA receptor function — receptors directly involved in learning, memory, and cognitive flexibility. It also plays a role in cortisol regulation, which is relevant because high morning cortisol can feel like anxiety or agitation rather than energy. Standard magnesium barely crosses the blood-brain barrier. Magnesium L-threonate does.

**The honest research position:** The strongest clinical evidence comes from animal models and studies in cognitively declining older adults. Human trials in healthy adults are more limited and show effects that are cumulative rather than acute. A single dose may not deliver a dramatically felt effect in a healthy 35-year-old. What it does do — consistently across the available evidence — is support the biochemical conditions for the other ingredients to work better. Think of it as priming the substrate rather than pulling a lever.

**Why include it given that caveat:** Two reasons. First, the mechanism is real even if the single-dose felt effect is modest. Second, this is a product people will take daily — cumulative benefit is legitimate benefit. The daily ritual is part of the product design. This ingredient rewards that.

**What to say vs. what not to say:** Magnesium has permitted health claims in the UK: "contributes to normal psychological function," "contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue," "contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism." These are legally usable. "Crosses the blood-brain barrier for cognitive enhancement" is not a permitted claim — even though the mechanism is real. The form — L-threonate — is where the differentiation lives. Explain it simply once, don't repeat it as a claim.

**Dose range:** 1,500–2,000mg of magnesium L-threonate (yielding approximately 144–195mg elemental magnesium). Note: this is relatively bulky and needs to be factored into sachet capacity calculations with the formulation manufacturer.

**Label language:** "Magnesium L-threonate" — sounds precise and considered. Should be accompanied by a simple one-line explanation on the product page: "The form of magnesium that actually reaches the brain."

### 5. BioPerine (Black Pepper Extract)
**The amplifier. Makes everything else work harder. Also the kick.**

> **In plain terms:** Makes every other ingredient absorb better. Also responsible for the mild peppery warmth you feel when you drink it — the signal that something's actually happening.

**What it is:** Standardised extract from black pepper (*Piper nigrum*), minimum 95% piperine. BioPerine is the patented, clinically tested form — this specific form is the one with the research, not generic black pepper extract.

**What it does — job one:** Piperine inhibits certain metabolic enzymes and transport proteins that would otherwise break down or clear other nutrients before they're absorbed. The result: measurably increased bioavailability of other ingredients taken at the same time. Studies show it can increase absorption of certain nutrients by 20–60%. In a formula where every ingredient needs to be absorbed efficiently to justify its dose, this matters significantly.

**What it does — job two:** Delivers a mild warming, peppery sensation — felt in the throat and stomach within minutes of drinking. This is the sensory cue Dan specified: the Listerine principle. The sensation primes the nervous system to interpret what follows as activation. This isn't manipulation — it's psychophysiology. The body uses sensory signals to anticipate and prepare for physiological change. The kick is real, the mechanism is real, and it serves both a functional and a perceptual role simultaneously.

**One important note:** Piperine enhances caffeine absorption too. At low doses this is a benefit — better caffeine uptake means consistent effect at a lower total caffeine dose. At very high caffeine doses it could push the stimulation curve upward rather than smooth it. The formulation manufacturer needs to account for this in calibrating the caffeine dose. Likely means erring on the lower end of the caffeine range.

**The research:** BioPerine specifically has been studied on bioavailability enhancement in multiple human trials. The sensory activation / psychophysiological priming mechanism is established in behavioural science (see: sensory-predicted efficacy literature).

**Dose:** 5mg. That's it. Small dose, significant effect.

**Label language:** "Black pepper extract" reads completely naturally. BioPerine as a named ingredient can appear in product detail as a quality signal — it's a recognisable branded ingredient to the informed consumer.

### 6. Vitamin B12 (as Methylcobalamin) and Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)
**The energy metabolism support. The insurance policy that also earns its place on the label.**

> **In plain terms:** Keeps the engine running underneath everything else. Supports how your cells actually produce energy — and more people are low in B12 than they realise, which quietly drags on how sharp and energised you feel.

**What they are:** Two specific B vitamins, chosen deliberately from the broader B complex family.

**B12 as methylcobalamin:** The active form your body uses directly. Standard B12 (cyanocobalamin) has to be converted — a conversion step a significant minority of people can't perform efficiently due to MTHFR gene variants. Methylcobalamin bypasses this entirely. B12 is directly involved in neurological function, red blood cell formation, and energy metabolism. Deficiency — which is more common than most people realise, particularly in people who eat less red meat — causes genuine fatigue and cognitive sluggishness.

**B5 (pantothenic acid):** A cofactor in coenzyme A synthesis, which sits directly in the ATP production pathway — how every cell in the body makes energy. Less headline-grabbing than B12, but mechanistically clean.

**The honest position on efficacy:** In genuinely deficient people, these ingredients move the needle measurably. In non-deficient people, the acute single-dose effect is modest. They're not the reason someone feels the product working within the first 45 minutes. They're contributing to energy metabolism at the cellular level — background infrastructure rather than front-of-stage performance.

**Why include them:** Two reasons. First, subclinical deficiency (not catastrophically low, just not optimal) is common enough that these genuinely help a meaningful portion of the audience. Second, B12 and B5 have well-established permitted health claims in the UK, which gives the product legitimate claim territory beyond what the more interesting but legally constrained ingredients can provide.

**Permitted claims (GB NHC Register):**
- B12: "contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism," "contributes to normal psychological function," "contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"
- B5: "contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism," "contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"

**Dose:** B12 at 2.5–10mcg (meaningfully above the 2.5mcg NRV to justify presence). B5 at 6mg+. Both at negligible cost per sachet.

**Label language:** "Vitamin B12 as methylcobalamin" sounds considered and premium — the "as methylcobalamin" signals quality of form without needing explanation. "Pantothenic acid (B5)" is clean.

## How the Stack Works Together — The System Explained Simply

Here's the honest version of what happens when someone takes this in the morning:

**Within 15–30 minutes:** BioPerine begins enhancing absorption of everything else. The peppery sensation signals the body that something is incoming.

**30–60 minutes:** Caffeine from green tea extract starts to build. L-theanine simultaneously promotes alpha brainwave activity — the caffeine arrives but without the spike edge. The curve is smooth. You feel alert, not wired.

**45–90 minutes:** Bilberry anthocyanins are supporting blood flow — more oxygen reaching the brain as the cognitive ingredients are starting to work. Magnesium L-threonate is present in the neural environment, supporting NMDA receptor function.

**Background throughout:** B12 and B5 are supporting energy metabolism at the cellular level. Magnesium is contributing to cortisol regulation — the morning's stress response is softer than it would otherwise be.

**The net result:** Not a caffeine hit. Not a crash. A sustained, composed feeling of being switched on. Calm energy with a clear head.

**How this sits alongside coffee:** Coffee does what coffee does. This product doesn't try to replace it — it layers on top. Coffee delivers a fast spike. This adds a slower, steadier curve underneath it. L-theanine modulates the combined effect, smoothing the edges. Bilberry supports blood flow to the brain while the caffeine is working. Magnesium supports the neural environment. B12 and B5 support energy metabolism underneath all of it. The combined result is more sustained than coffee alone — not because there's more caffeine, but because everything around the caffeine is working better.

## What's Deliberately Not In This Formula

**Mushroom extracts (Lion's Mane, etc.):** Trendy. The mechanism (Nerve Growth Factor) is real but cumulative over weeks, not acute. Doesn't contribute to the felt morning experience. Left out on purpose.

**Electrolytes (sodium, magnesium, potassium blend):** The hydration-cognition link is real but only meaningful in dehydrated people. The audience is broadly hydrated. Electrolytes pull the brand toward sports drink territory. The drink is with water — hydration is contextual. Left out on purpose.

**Full B-complex:** Reads as a supermarket multivitamin. Two targeted, justified B vitamins reads as considered. The full complex would dilute the intentionality of the formula.

**Fats / fat-soluble compounds:** Formulation nightmare in cold water. Emulsification and stability issues make this impractical.

**Adaptogens (Ashwagandha, Rhodiola):** Legitimate mechanisms but cumulative rather than acute. Better suited to a separate evening or recovery product. Left out of the morning formula.

**Synthetic caffeine:** Faster absorbed, harder crash, less clean on a label.

## UK Regulatory Framework — What Can and Cannot Be Said

This section is critical. Everything below is referenced to the GB NHC Register (post-Brexit UK framework). This governs what can be stated on pack, in advertising, and on the website as a health claim.

**Permitted claims by ingredient:**

| Ingredient | Permitted claim territory |
|---|---|
| Caffeine (from green tea) | "Contributes to an increase in endurance performance" / "helps increase alertness" (at 75mg per serving) |
| Magnesium | "Contributes to normal psychological function" / "contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" |
| Vitamin B12 | "Contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism" / "normal psychological function" / "reduction of tiredness and fatigue" |
| Vitamin B5 | "Contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism" / "reduction of tiredness and fatigue" |

**No permitted claims:**

| Ingredient | Status |
|---|---|
| L-theanine | No permitted health claims in UK |
| Bilberry / anthocyanins | No permitted cognitive or blood flow claims in UK |
| BioPerine / piperine | No permitted claims in UK |
| EGCG | No permitted claims in UK |

**What this means in practice:**

The ingredients doing the most interesting brand and copy work — L-theanine, bilberry, BioPerine — are the ones with no permitted claim territory. You can explain their mechanism educationally on the website (this is different from making a health claim). You cannot state they "support focus," "improve blood flow to the brain," or "enhance absorption" on pack or in an ad without regulatory risk.

The permitted claims (caffeine for alertness, magnesium and B vitamins for tiredness and psychological function) are less exciting but legally solid. Build the regulated claim foundation on those. Build the brand story around the mechanism education for the rest.

**The line to stay on the right side of:**
- "Contains magnesium, which contributes to normal psychological function" ✅
- "The form of magnesium that reaches the brain" (educational, not a claim) ✅
- "L-theanine: found in tea leaves. Works with caffeine to smooth the energy curve." (educational) ✅
- "L-theanine supports calm focus" ❌
- "Bilberry improves blood flow to your brain" ❌

## Things to Never Make Up or Overstate

- Do not cite specific study numbers or academic paper titles without verification
- Do not state BioPerine increases absorption by a specific percentage without checking the specific study for the specific nutrient
- Do not claim magnesium L-threonate produces an acute felt effect in healthy adults — the evidence for this in that population is limited
- Do not transfer the NZ blackcurrant / CurraNZ cerebral blood flow research to bilberry specifically — the mechanism is shared, the specific research is not
- Do not claim L-theanine or bilberry produce effects on pack — explain the mechanism educationally only
